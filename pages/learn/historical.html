Historical Cyclones in Myanmar

Explore some of the major cyclones that have affected Myanmar, their impact on communities, and the lessons they have taught us about disaster preparedness.

29 APRIL 2006

Cyclone Mala

On 29 th April 2006 Cyclone Mala hit Myanmar near Gwa in Rakhine state causing strong winds and heavy rainfall. This caused damage to houses, trees and buildings. The local community suffered from extremely difficult situations.

Cyclone Mala has shown the importance of preparing for disasters. Families should have emergency items ready, make sure their houses are safe and know where to go if they need to evacuate. It is important to pay attention to weather warnings given from official sources and do not share false information. The community can make themselves less at risk by keeping them up to date with correct information and helping those who may be in need. This cyclone has shown us how important it is to prepare for emergencies and pay attention to weather warnings to keep people safe and reduce damage in the future.

2 MAY 2008

Cyclone Nargis

Cyclone Nargis occurred on May 2, 2008, affecting Myanmar with a great disaster. The weather pattern caused strong wind and torrential rainfall with severe storm surge. This caused damage in the Ayeyarwady Delta and Yangon. Villages in the coastal regions and areas which are low-lying flooded severely. Thousands of structures such as houses, schools, and hospitals were either damaged or completely destroyed. In addition, roads, bridges, power supply, and telecommunication services were adversely affected causing a challenge for aid delivery. People lost their lives and millions were affected by this disaster. Cyclone Nargis highlighted the importance of warning systems, supplies, evacuation planning, and safety shelters.

Thousands of homes, schools and buildings were destroyed, while roads and communication systems were damaged. Many people lost their lives, and millions were affected by the disaster. Cyclone Nargis showed the importance of early warnings, emergency supplies and safe shelters to protect communities from future cyclones.

OCTOBER 2010

Cyclone Giri

The Cyclone Giri hit the Rakhine State in October 2010, and brought very strong winds, rainfall, and floods in the nearby towns and villages. This storm destroyed many houses, trees, and buildings. People had to deal with very challenging conditions because of the damages from the cyclone. There were damages to roads and other infrastructure, which made it more difficult for people to travel, communicate and get help.

People lost their homes and needed help after the cyclone. The Cyclone Giri resulted in 45 fatalities and created many challenges for people. This storm demonstrated how quickly the strong storm can impact coastal communities. The preparation of emergency supplies, securing the homes, following the weather reports, and finding a place to hide before the cyclone are very important.

14 MAY 2023

Cyclone Mocha

Cyclone Mocha made landfall near Sittwe in Rakhine State on 14 May 2023, bringing extremely strong winds, heavy rainfall and severe flooding to many parts of Myanmar. It was one of the strongest cyclones to affect the country in recent years. Thousands of houses, schools, hospitals and other buildings were damaged or destroyed. Many trees were destroyed, while electricity lines, roads and communication systems were also affected. Floodwater covered several areas, making it difficult for people to travel, access clean drinking water and receive emergency assistance. Many families had to leave their homes and move to safer shelters. Cyclone Mocha highlighted the importance of strong buildings, early evacuation, emergency supplies and reliable weather warnings. It reminded communities that preparing before a cyclone arrives can help protect lives and reduce damage.

Many trees were knocked down, and power lines and roads were also damaged. Some areas were covered by floodwater, making it difficult for people to travel and get clean water. Many families had to leave their homes and move to safe shelters. Cyclone Mocha showed why strong homes, early warnings and emergency supplies are important during a cyclone.

What can we learn from these cyclones?

Each cyclone reminds us that preparation matters. Understanding weather warnings, keeping emergency supplies, knowing evacuation routes and finding safe shelters can help communities reduce risks and protect lives.
