Historical Cyclones in Myanmar

Explore some of the major cyclones that have affected Myanmar, their impact on communities, and the lessons they have taught us about disaster preparedness.

29 APRIL 2006

Cyclone Mala

Cyclone Mala struck Myanmar near Gwa in Rakhine State on 29 April 2006. It brought very strong winds and heavy rainfall, causing damage to homes, trees and important infrastructure. The storm affected local communities and showed how dangerous cyclones can be for people living near coastal areas.

It also highlighted the importance of preparing emergency supplies, securing homes and following official weather warnings. Cyclone Mala reminds us that preparing early can help protect lives and reduce damage during future storms.

2 MAY 2008

Cyclone Nargis

Cyclone Nargis struck Myanmar on 2 May 2008, causing one of the country's worst natural disasters. It brought powerful winds, heavy rainfall and a devastating storm surge, severely affecting the Ayeyarwady Delta and Yangon areas.

Thousands of homes, schools and buildings were destroyed, while roads and communication systems were damaged. Many people lost their lives, and millions were affected by the disaster. Cyclone Nargis showed the importance of early warnings, emergency supplies and safe shelters to protect communities from future cyclones.

OCTOBER 2010

Cyclone Giri

Cyclone Giri struck Rakhine State in October 2010. It brought very strong winds and heavy rain, which damaged many houses, trees and buildings. Many people in nearby towns and villages were affected by the storm.

Some families lost their homes and needed help after the cyclone. Roads and other important places were also damaged, making life difficult for local people. The cyclone caused 45 deaths and left many communities in need of support. Cyclone Giri showed why people should prepare early, listen to weather warnings and find safe places before a cyclone arrives.

14 MAY 2023

Cyclone Mocha

Cyclone Mocha made landfall near Sittwe in Rakhine State on 14 May 2023. It brought very strong winds, heavy rain and serious flooding to many parts of Myanmar. The storm damaged thousands of houses, schools, hospitals and other buildings.

Many trees were knocked down, and power lines and roads were also damaged. Some areas were covered by floodwater, making it difficult for people to travel and get clean water. Many families had to leave their homes and move to safe shelters. Cyclone Mocha showed why strong homes, early warnings and emergency supplies are important during a cyclone.

What can we learn from these cyclones?

Each cyclone reminds us that preparation matters. Understanding weather warnings, keeping emergency supplies, knowing evacuation routes and finding safe shelters can help communities reduce risks and protect lives.
