How and Why Cyclones Form

Discover how warm ocean water, rising air and rotating winds work together to create powerful tropical storms.

Learn How They Form

WHAT IS A CYCLONE?

A Powerful Storm Over Warm Oceans

A cyclone is a large rotating storm that forms over warm ocean water. It can bring strong winds, heavy rain and flooding. Cyclones get their energy from warm, moist air above the ocean. They can become very dangerous when they grow stronger and move towards land.A cyclone is an enormous storm system that forms over the warm waters of the ocean. It causes strong winds, heavy rainfall, thunderstorms, and floods in the surrounding areas. Cyclones are natural weather systems with great force that cause destruction to houses, buildings, roads, and the environment. These storms derive their power from warm and humid air in the ocean. When the ocean temperatures are warm enough, the ocean becomes the source of warmth and humidity for the formation of a cyclone. The winds and clouds grow stronger as the storm develops.

How Do Cyclones Form?

1. WARM OCEAN WATER

The Ocean Heats the Air

Cyclones normally develop over warm ocean waters in the tropics. The sea surface should be 26.5°C or warmer in order to provide enough warmth and moisture that will help the development of a cyclone. Warm ocean surface heats the air above it, causing the air to become warm and humid. Such an atmosphere is the source of energy that helps a storm develop. As warm air goes up, there develops a low pressure zone at the surface level, and air rushes towards it, providing more moisture. If the correct atmospheric conditions persist, then a tropical cyclone may develop gradually.

2. RISING AIR

Warm Air Rises

Ocean water heats the air above it, making the warm, moist air light enough for it to rise up into the sky. This is referred to as convection. In rising, the warm air meets colder parts of the air and water vapor cools to form tiny droplets that combine to form clouds. In addition, there is continuous supply of warm, moist air from the ocean surface to replace the rising air. The cycle continues, resulting in the storm gathering moisture and energy from the ocean and gaining power.

2. CLOUD FORMATION

Clouds and Rainstorms Develop

As warm, moist air rises, tall clouds and rainstorms develop. Water vapor in the air cools to form tiny droplets in the process known as condensation. Condensation results in the release of heat into the air around the storm. Thus, the storm gains more energy and continues growing. More heat is released resulting in the rise of more warm, moist air and entry of more moisture into the storm. Thus, there is a cycle of rising air, condensation, and cloud formation. As the thunderstorms organize themselves, the cyclone inside them strengthens.

4. ROTATING WINDS

The Storm Begins to Rotate

Rising of warm air results in a low-pressure area near the ocean surface. Air from other areas moves in to replace the rising air. However, due to the rotation of the Earth, the moving air curves. This phenomenon is called the Coriolis effect. The rotation of cyclones results from the Coriolis effect. In the Northern hemisphere, cyclones rotate counterclockwise while those in the Southern hemisphere rotate clockwise. Due to continued movement of air towards the center, the storm organizes itself into a rotating system.

5. CYCLONE DEVELOPMENT

The Cyclone Grows Stronger

If the storm has sufficient warm ocean water, moist air, and proper atmospheric conditions, it will continue strengthening itself. There is increased entry of moisture into the storm, organization of thunderstorms and increase in wind speed. With decrease in the air pressure at the center, stronger winds form around the low-pressure area. The storm passes through stages. It begins as a tropical disturbance, then tropical depression, tropical storm and finally tropical cyclone. Powerful cyclones have an eye and an eyewall. The eye is the calm center while the eyewall consists of powerful storms. At this stage, the cyclone can have very strong winds, heavy rainfall and storm surges. The danger of the cyclone is greatly increased when it approaches coastal areas.

Remember

Cyclones form through a combination of warm ocean water, moist air and rotating winds. Understanding how they develop helps us learn why they can become dangerous and why preparing early is important.
