Reducing
Cyclone Risks

Small actions can make a big difference. Learn how to prepare for cyclones and protect lives.

Explore Safety Tips

WHY IT MATTERS

Preparation starts before the storm.

Preparing early helps communities get ready for strong winds, heavy rain and floods. People can prepare emergency bags, store food and clean water, protect their homes, and plan where to go if they need to leave. They can also listen to weather news and follow safety advice. This helps keep people safe and reduces damage to homes.

1. Protect Your Houses

Build Strong Houses

Strong houses can help protect families from the powerful winds and heavy rain brought by cyclones. Roofs should be securely attached, while walls, doors and windows should be checked and repaired before a storm arrives. Loose roof sheets, broken windows and weak doors can become dangerous when strong winds hit. People should also bring in or safely secure outdoor objects such as chairs, bins, tools and plant pots because these items can be blown away and cause damage. Where possible, families should ask qualified builders about ways to strengthen their homes. Regularly checking the house and fixing small problems early can reduce damage and make homes safer. Stronger buildings can help communities recover more easily after a cyclone.

2. Be Prepared

Prepare Supplies

Keeping emergency supplies ready before a cyclone arrives is important for protecting your family. Prepare enough drinking water, canned or dried food, medicine, a first-aid kit, a torch, spare batteries and a fully charged power bank. These items can help you manage if electricity, clean water or transportation services become unavailable. It is also useful to prepare important documents, mobile phones, extra clothing and basic hygiene products. Store everything in a waterproof bag or container and keep it somewhere safe, dry and easy to reach. Make sure everyone in your family knows where the emergency supplies are kept. Preparing these items early can make it easier to stay safe and comfortable during and after a cyclone.

3. Prevent Flooding

Keep Drains Clean

Keeping drains clean is an important way to reduce flooding during heavy rainfall and cyclones. Rubbish, plastic bags, leaves and other waste should be thrown into rubbish bins instead of being left on streets or dumped into drains. When drains become blocked, rainwater cannot flow away properly, causing water to build up and flood roads, houses and nearby communities. Before the rainy season begins, people should help clean drains around their homes and neighbourhoods. Communities can also organise regular cleaning activities to remove rubbish and prevent blockages. It is important to dispose of waste properly and avoid throwing anything into waterways. Keeping drains clear allows rainwater to flow more easily, reduces the risk of flooding and helps create a cleaner and safer environment for everyone.

4. Protect Trees

Protect Mangroves

Another method that could be used to minimize the impact of cyclones on coastal populations is the protection of mangroves. Mangrove trees are found in coastal regions, and their roots have the ability to have soil and prevent erosion. In case of storms, the mangrove forest acts as a barrier that can absorb some of the impact of the waves. They also offer shelter to fish, crabs, birds and other species. Coastal communities could help by ensuring that mangroves are not destroyed, growing native trees in appropriate locations and making sure that there is no trash in the coastal forests. While mangroves do not prevent all storms or floods, healthy mangrove forests act as a natural buffer.
